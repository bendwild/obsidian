General theme: addressing the tension between market-driven pharmaceutical manufacturing and the fundamental right to health in a fragile context


What gets made =/= what is needed
Crowded shelves of simple generics, simple registrations of paracetamol, last-generation antibiotics (fueling resistance)
Empty shelves of monoclonal antibodies, insulin, vaccines and blood products

Fixed prices controls
- so complex medicines dont pay
- and manufactures shift production to things that are profitable under price cap

When quality doesn't pay
- thin oversight
- good manifuctaring practice on paper
- weak testing
- same certificate for all
- marketing over quality

And most of all patients don't see the difference

The result two tiers of care: 
- for the few: imported or Lebanese brands of trusted quality, paid out of pocket
- for the majority: medicines of unassured quality, stock-outs, and skipped doses

Three pillars for reform: 
- regulatory harmonisation
- strategic localisation
- policy reform
	- one overarching pharmaceutical strategy
	- pricing, registration and marketing rules that reward quality
	- retain skilled staff
	- encourage procurement of quality local medicine


> [!attention] Private capacity =/= public health security
> In a polycrisis, commercial logic favours profit over equity, unless the state steers incentives and oversight

We need a market where making good medicines is also good business
