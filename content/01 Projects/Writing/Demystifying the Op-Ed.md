# The recipe
1. Identify a compelling issue
	1. What’s my story?
	2. What am I passionate about?
	3. **Why am I the right person to tell this?**
2. Craft your message
	1. Hone in on a few key points
	2. Think about your audience
	3. Tell the story with strong, lively language
	4. Use personal experience, an anecdote or examples to illustrate your point
	5. Have a strong, maybe controversial, **opinion**
3. Know your goal
	1. Peg your piece to a **timely** policy angle, news event or other point that underscores why now is the right time to tell this story
	2. Propose a solution or a **call to action**

# Tips for Telling Your Story

> It’s all about structure! And keep it simple!

- **The “lede”:** Draw the reader in with a compelling anecdote or a short, punchy, provocative paragraph. Make me want to read the next paragraph. 
- **The top:** Tell the important stuff first. Why does this matter? What’s the context? Why are you the perfect person to tell this story. 
- **The body:** You really can only make three points. And possibly a rebuttal to address a logical argument. Keep your paragraphs short, and use active, concise language. Be persuasive. 
- **The ending:** Drive home your point without repeating yourself. What’s your solution. Is there a call to action. 

# Potential pitfalls
- Using jargon. Don’t. Same with acronyms. Keep at a 10th grade level. 
- Bogging down the piece with too many facts and figures. A little goes a long way. 
- Ranting into the wind. If you are focusing on a problem, propose a solution. If you ask a question, answer it. No rambling. 
- Not following submission guidelines. Provide them with everything they request. News outlets get tons of submissions. Don’t be afraid to check up. 
- Be brutal in your writing. Know what to cut.
