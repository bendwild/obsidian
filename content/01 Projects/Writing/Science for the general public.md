> Writing *is* public health. It is what makes public health *public*. Public health works lives or dies with communication.

Writing is the bridge between knowledge and understanding, by utilizing:
- **trust** (internet as truth, 60% of adults in US used the internet in the past 12 months)
- **clarity** (low health literacy, 60% of US adults demonstrated inadequate health literacy)
- **action** (poorer outcomes, health literacy predicts health status more strongly than income or education)

> AI is a writing assistant, not a scientific authority and not a (good) writer

# 1. Identify the one most important idea
- 8-12 plain words
- nuance comes after, mainly charting the story right now
- what don’t you need right now

*What is long covid*
# 2. Define your audience’s needs
- personal impact
- public impact
- what might clinicians need to know
- what about policy makers

What is the *angle* that will capture your audience’s attention. 

*How to recognize long covid?*
*How do you get long covid?*
*How to act?*

# 3. The data: remove barriers to understanding
- jargon 
- statistics
- visuals
- emotions

How can you make the science *understandable* and *usable*?

> [!info] How do we handle jargon
> - 6-word definition
> - describe the effect (skip the term, explain what happens)
> - everyday analogy and metaphors: “immune off-switch stuck”, “blood traffic jam”
> - split long sentences, one idea per sentence
> - turn nouns into verbs: move from abstraction toward action (don‘t use passive voice )
