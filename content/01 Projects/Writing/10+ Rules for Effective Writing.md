1. **Writing is hard.** Devote sufficient time and attention to it in your schedule. Same for editing, re-writing, and getting peer feedback.  
2. **Less is more**. Leave them wanting more. 
3. **Use the active voice**. The subject is performing an action. 
4. **Establish your point of view**. A reader needs to be situated in an issue or topic. Establishing a point of view engages the reader. Before outlining, ask yourself what you actually think about this topic. 
5. **Consider the audience you are writing for.** How do they tend to consume information? What do they care most about? Remember you are educating and entertaining. 
6. **Create an outline before you write.** An outline creates the skeleton. 
7. **Use short sentences.** Good writing is as much about *pacing* as about content. Also, try to make a good mix of length of sentences. There is a certain musicality to writing. If you write a long sentence, follow it with a short one. 
8. **Don’t bury the lede.** This is taken right from journalism and storytelling. Include your most important points up front in case the reader scans. Leave out irrelevant details. It’s not just an afterthought, it sets up the tone for everything. (BLUF = bottom line up front)
9. **Verbs are the mitochondria of writing.** 
10. **Delete unnecessary words.** If you’re stuck when writing or editing, go through and cut adverbs. It’s a good way to feel productive and tighten your sentences. 
11. **Examples are key.** How do you make an issue clear and concrete? Examples help the reader bridge a knowledge gap. Pretend you are talking to a kid (but why).
12. **Jargon and acronyms signal insider status**. Even worse, they get in the way reducing comprehension. You are aiming for clarity. Reduce or remove whenever possible. 
13. **Write a story and hold on to your voice.** Writing is becoming more and more formulaic (AI).
14. **Bullets and subheads are your friends.** 
15. **Spend time crafting strong transitions.** They, too, help move the reader along. This is super important when you’re writing a blog, a memo and other forms of writing. Take note of writers/articles where this is really well done and think about why this is good.
16. **Read your work aloud and share it with friends**. Where is it dragging and incomprehensible?
