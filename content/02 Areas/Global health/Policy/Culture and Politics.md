# Reflections
Social movements in US are seen as to emerge in response to the expansion of social opportunities that grant formal social movement organizations (SMOs) and movement entrepreneurs the opportunity to engage in successful “resource mobilization”

Culture, in all of its manifestations, was rarely invoked in the emergence and development of social movements, until recently

European, “new social movement” perspective, which make cultural and cognitive factors central to the study of social movements

Ways in which culture can facilitate movement emergence
- framing as an act of cultural appropriation (MLK, student democracy movement in Beijing)
- expanding cultural opportunities as a stimulus to action
- ideological or cultural contradictions
- suddenly imposed grievances as cognitive stimulus (George Floyd f.e., dramatic, highly publicized and generally unexpected events)
- dramatizations of system vulnerability
- the availability of master frames (legitimizing collective action)

Expanding political opportunities do not produce social movement, they only offer insurgents a certain “structural potential” for collective action

The role of long-standing activist subcultures in movement emergence

Mediating between opportunities and concrete mobilization efforts are the shared meanings people bring to their lives. These meanings, in turn, are expected to be shaped by the cultural resources and opportunities mentioned above. 

Movements will: 
- bear the imprint of the broader cultural context(s) in which it is embedded
- reshape broader cultural contours of mainstream society

Typically movements have a social locus (generational strata, geographic location…)
- it is not uncommon for the locus of protest activity to shift/expand over the life of a movement
- as such shifts occur, also shift in ideational and material culture of the movement

Movement-based cultural change is often overlooked
- creation of new collective identities
- force for innovation in strategic action forms
- source of new material cultural items

4 factors mediating the cultural consequences of social movements: 
- the extensiveness of the movement’s goals (the more extensive, the more likely it will be a force for cultural change)
- the movement’s success in attaining those goals
- the extent to which the movement results in prolonged and meaningful contact between two previously segregated groups
	- potential to produce a new hybrid based on two subcultures (f.e. Christianity)
- the extent of the movement’s access to existing cultural elites in society
