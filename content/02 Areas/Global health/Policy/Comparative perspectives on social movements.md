# Reflections

[[@ Daniel Bell]] - “The end of ideology” (haha psych)

Social movements are shaped by the broader set of political constraints and opportunities unique to the national context in which they are embedded

Resource mobilization theory
- break from grievance-based conceptions
- focus on the mobilization process and the formal organizational manifestations of these processes

Political process model
- stresses the crucial importance of expanding political opportunities as the ultimate spur to collective action

What are the dynamic relations among opportunities, mobilizing structures, and framing processes?

No matter how momentous a change appears in retrospect, it only becomes an “opportunity” when defined as such by a group of actors sufficiently well organized to act on this shared definition of the situation

[[Fundamental attribution error]]

The ways in which changes in one or more of these dimensions render the political system more receptive or vulnerable to challenge by insurgent groups VS is collective action structured by the available political opportunity

Gamson finds that groups which used force and violence against their opponents tended to be more successful than groups that did not

Tarrow argues that it is disruption, or the threat thereof, that grants movements their improbable effectiveness as vehicles of social change

Ironically, pressure from extremists may simultaneously push moderates to adopt more radical positions themselves

Groups whose goals required the displacement of their opponents were much less likely to be successful

Movements may largely be born of environmental opportunities, but their fate is heavily shaped by their own actions
