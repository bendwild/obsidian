Rather  than start with a policy decision, these  'bottom- uppers' started with an analysis of the multitude of actors who interact at the operational  (local) level on a particular problem or issue.

## Top down approaches: a not entirely disinterested evaluation
> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=2&annotation=257R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.2]]
> > The essential features of a top down approach are that it starts with a policy decision by governmental (often central government) officials

Six sufficient and generally necessary conditions for the effective implementation of legal objectives: 
1. Clear and consistent objecives
2. Adequate causal theory
3. Implementation process legally structured
4. Committed and skilful implementing officials
5. Support of interest groups and sovereigns
6. Changes in socio-economic conditions which do not substantially undermine political support or causal theory

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=5&annotation=260R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.5]]
> > In short, the first three conditions can be dealt with by the initial policy decision (e.g. a statute), whereas the latter three are largely the product of subsequent political and economic pressure during the implementation process
> 
> 

> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=5&annotation=263R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.5]]
> > In short, while Sabatier and Mazmanian  rejected  hierarchical  control  -  in  the  sense  of  tightly constrained behavior - as impossible, they argued that the behavior of street level bureaucrats and target groups could be kept within acceptable bounds  over  time  if  the  six  conditions  were  met

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=7&annotation=269R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.7]]
> > First, the importance it attaches to legal structuring of the implementa- tion process -  one of its major  innovations -  has been confirmed  in numerous studies.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=7&annotation=266R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.7]]
> > Likewise, two of the major contributions borrowed from Pressman and Wildavsky (1973) - veto points and causal theory - were confirmed in many studies.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=8&annotation=272R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.8]]
> > Second, the six conditions of effective implementation have proven to be a useful  checklist of critical factors in understanding variations in program performance and in understanding the strategies of program proponents over time.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=9&annotation=275R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.9]]
> > First,  the  emphasis  they  placed  on  'clear  and  consistent  policy objectives' was a mistake

> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=10&annotation=278R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.10]]
> > The fundamental flaw in top-down models, according to Hjern and Hull (1982),  Hanf  (1982),  Barrett and Fudge (1981), Elmore (1979) and other bottom-uppers,  is  that  they  start  from  the  perspective  of  (central) decision-makers and thus tend to neglect other actors.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=10&annotation=281R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.10]]
> > A second, and related, criticism of top-down models is that they are difficult to use in situations where there is no dominant policy (statute) or agency, but rather a multitude of governmental directives and actors, none of them preeminent

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=10&annotation=284R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.10]]
> > A third criticism of top-down models is that they are likely to ignore, or at least underestimate, the strategies used by street level bureaucrats and target groups to get around (central) policy and/or to divert it to their own purposes
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=11&annotation=287R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.11]]
> > Finally, there are a whole series of arguments that the distinction between policy formulation  and  policy implementation  is misleading and/or useless

# Bottom-up approaches: the Promised Land?
> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=12&annotation=290R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.12]]
> > In contrast to the top-down approach -  which starts from a policy decision and focuses on the extent to which its objectives are attained over time and why - the bottom-up approach of Hjern et al. starts by identi- fying the network of actors involved in [[service delivery]] in one or more local areas and asks them about their goals, strategies, activities, and contacts.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=13&annotation=293R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.13]]
> > First, they have developed an explicit and replicable methodology for identifying  a  policy network ('implementation structure  1  )

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=14&annotation=296R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.14]]
> > Second, because Hjern et  al.  do not begin with  a  governmental program but rather with actors' perceived problems and the strategies developed for dealing with them, they are able to assess the relative importance of a variety of governmental programs vis-a-vis private organizations and market  forces  in  solving  those problems.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=14&annotation=299R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.14]]
> > Third, because Hjern et al. do not start with a focus on the attainment of formal policy objectives, they are free to see all sorts of (unintended) consequences of governmental and private program
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=14&annotation=302R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.14]]
> > Fourth, this approach is able to deal with a policy/problem area involving a multitude of public (and private) programs, none of them preeminent.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=14&annotation=305R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.14]]
> > Finally, because of  their focus on the strategies pursued by a wide range of actors, bottom-uppers are better able to deal with strategic interaction over time than are top-downers - who tend to focus on the strategies of program proponents, while neglecting those of other actors
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=14&annotation=308R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.14]]
> > First, just  as top-downers  are in danger of overemphasizing  the importance of  the  Center vis-a-vis the Periphery, bottom-uppers are likely to overemphasize the ability of the Periphery to frustrate the Center.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=15&annotation=311R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.15]]
> > Second, in a related point, Hjern et al. take the present participants in an implementation structure as given without examining the prior efforts of various individuals to affect  participation rates.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=15&annotation=314R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.15]]
> > Its failure to start from an explicit theory of the factors affecting its subject of  interest. Because it relies very heavily on the perceptions  and  activities  of participants,  it is their  prisoner -  and therefore is unlikely to analyze the factors  indirectly  affecting their behavior or even the factors directly affecting such behavior which the participants do not recognize.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=15&annotation=317R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.15]]
> > Top-downers have been  preoccupied with (a) the effectiveness of specific governmental programs and (b) the ability of elected officials  to guide and constrain the behavior of civil servants and target groups.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=16&annotation=320R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.16]]
> >  Bottom-uppers, on the other hand, are far less preoccupied with the extent to which a formally enacted policy decision is carried out and much more concerned with accurately mapping the strategies of actors concerned with a policy problem

## Where do we go from here?
Comparative advantage: 

| Top-down approach                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Bottom-up approach                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Useful in cases where there is a dominant public program or where the analyst is solely interested in the effectiveness of a program                                                                                       | Useful when a multitude of public and private actors are involved                                                                                    |
| Useful in making a preliminary assessment of which approach to use or in cases where scores on the six conditions are moderate/mixed                                                                                       | In case where the scores on the six conditions are relatively low and one is interested in inter-local variation                                     |
| Focuses on the extent to which the overall system is structured/constrained                                                                                                                                                | Focuses on local implementation structures and thus better for assessing the dynamics of local variation                                             |
| Advantage when: <br>- dominant piece of legislation structuring the situation<br>- research funds are limited<br>- one is primarily interested in mean responses<br>- the situation is structured at least moderately well | Advantage when: <br>- large number of actors without power dependency<br>- one is primarily interested in the dynamics of different local situations |

## An advocacy coalition framework of policy change
How to merge both approaches? = advocacy coalition framework of policy change

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=19&annotation=326R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.19]]
> > Consistent with the bottom-uppers, one needs to start from a policy problem or subsystem - rather than a law or other policy  decision  -  and then examine the strategies employed by relevant actors in both the public and private sectors at various levels of government as they attempt to deal with the issue  consistent  with  their  objectives.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=19&annotation=334R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.19]]
> > Likewise, the concerns of top-down theorists with the manner in which legal and socio-economic factors structure behavioral options need to be incorporated into the synthesis, as do their concerns with the validity of the causal assumptions behind specific programs and strategies

Focus on: 
1. The effects of socio-economic (and other) changes external to the policy network/subsystem on actors’ resources and strategies
2. The attempts by various actors to manipulate the legal attributes of governmental programs in order to achieve their objectives over time
3. Actors’ efforts to improve their understanding of the magnitude and factors affecting the problem, as well as the impacts of various policy instruments

> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=19&annotation=337R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.19]]
> > Attention thus shifts from policy implementation to policy change over periods of 10-20 years. The longer time span creates, however, a need to aggregate actors into a manageable number of groups if the researcher is to avoid severe information overload.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research.pdf#page=19&annotation=340R|Sabatier PA. Top-down and Bottom-up Approaches to Implementation Research, p.19]]
> > In short, the synthesis adopts the bottom-uppers' unit of analysis - a whole variety of public and private actors involved with a policy problem —  as well as their concerns with understanding  the perspectives  and strategies  of  all  major  categories  of  actors  (not  simply  program proponents).  It  then  combines  this starting point with  top-downers' concerns with the manner in which socio-economic conditions and legal instruments constrain behavior.
