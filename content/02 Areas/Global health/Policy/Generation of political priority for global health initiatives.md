# Reflections
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority.pdf#page=1&annotation=154R|Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority, p.1370]]
> > We propose an initial framework for analysing the determinants  of  political  priority  for  global  health initiatives, and we hope future researchers will modify and improve this framework. It consists of four categories: the power of actors involved, the ideas they use to portray the issue, the nature of the political contexts in which they operate, and characteristics of the issue itself

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority.pdf#page=2&annotation=157R|Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority, p.1371]]
> > Any issue can be framed in several ways. For example, [[HIV]]/AIDS has been framed as a public-health problem, a development issue, a humanitarian crisis, and a threat to security.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority.pdf#page=3&annotation=160R|Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority, p.1372]]
> > Many elements of political context matter, but two are essential. First are policy windows (factor 7), which are moments  in  time  when  worldwide  conditions  align favourably  for  an  issue,  presenting  advocates  with especially strong opportunities to reach international and national political leaders.  1

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority.pdf#page=3&annotation=163R|Shiffman and Smith Generation of Political Priority, p.1372]]
> > A second crucial  element  of  context  is  the  global  governance structure for the sector (factor 8)—ie, the set of norms (shared  beliefs  on  appropriate  behaviour)  and  the institutions that negotiate and enforce these norms.
