> > we argue that although science should inform health policy, it cannot be the only consideration

## How science should inform health policy
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=1&annotation=83R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.1]]
> > Science can and must inform health policy decisions in several ways, including by identifying emerging problems.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=1&annotation=86R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.1]]
> > Science can also be useful for telling policy makers which tools are likely to produce a desired effect.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=1&annotation=89R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.1]]
> > Economic research provides further help for policy makers by allowing them to determine the cost and cost effectiveness of interventions.

## Role of factors other than scientific evidence
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=1&annotation=92R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.1]]
> > Science also has limits in health policy decision making. For example, effective policy on smoking required more than the discovery that it is a cause of lung cancer and other diseases

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=1&annotation=95R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.1]]
> > The decision to form policy in response to evidence of effectiveness is made politically, not least by voters who selected particular individuals to lead their countries.

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=2&annotation=98R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.2]]
> > More generally, societies have values that don’t need to be proved in randomised controlled trials and are appropriate over-riding considerations in policy.

## Scientists as human beings
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=2&annotation=101R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.2]]
> > Firstly, scientists, like all people, are wrong at least some of the time. The scientific “fact” that seems a certain basis for policy in one era may be overturned by new discoveries or new research methods

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=2&annotation=104R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.2]]
> > Secondly, again like all people, scientists can confuse their opinions on policy matters with objective facts.
> 
> 

> [!PDF|187, 97, 229] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=2&annotation=107R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.2]]
> > Conflating facts and values allows scientists to use their authority inappropriately—that is, to cloak their effort to make society live by their values as a disinterested, objective, and unassailable stance. This may lead the public to defer to scientists on the assumption that they know better, but in a democracy there can be no experts on values.

## Conclusion
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy.pdf#page=2&annotation=110R|Humphreys K, Piot P. Scientific evidence alone is not sufficient basis for health policy, p.2]]
> > Scientific research is an extremely valuable tool for informing health policy decisions because it can identify emerging problems, offer tools to tackle those problems, and forecast the likely effect of various policy choices. This potential to inform does not mean that democratic and human rights considerations can or should be cast aside, hence there is no such thing as evidence based policy, only “evidence informed” policy.
