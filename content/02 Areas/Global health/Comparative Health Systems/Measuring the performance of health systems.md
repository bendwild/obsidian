> We often compare the health of populations and countries by examining indicators such as life expectancy, infant mortality, or the incidence of specific diseases. However, these measures may be more influenced social and economic factors such as education, inequality and behaviors. The health system certainly plays its part in influencing health outcomes of these indicators, mainly through financing the treatment of diseases and ensuring the quality of care,  **but other factors have a greater influence.** 

# Health system performance assessment (HSPA)

> = evaluating how well a system achieves its goals given its resources

> [!INFO] Performance is produced by the system
> - Functions → Intermediate objectives → Final goals
> - Focus on outcomes, not just inputs

|                      | Quality                                                                                                             | Coverage                                                                                                                                                    | Financial protection                                                                                                                                | Efficiency                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Central question** | Are services effective, safe, and person-centered?                                                                  | Do people receive the services they need? (not just whether the service exists in principle)                                                                | Are people protected from catastrophic costs? (people do not need to choose between basic needs and healthcare)                                     | Is the system getting value from its resources? (maximum possible health outcomes given available resources)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Key concepts**     | Amenable mortality: <br>- deaths from conditions that should not be fatal in the presence of timely, effective care | UHC service coverage index: <br>- a composite of 14 tracer indicators across reproductive health, child health, infectious disease, and NCDs (scaled 0-100) | Catastrophic expenditure: <br>- households spending more than 10% of total consumption on health<br><br>WHO threshold:<br>- OOP above 30-40% of CHE | Technical efficiency: <br>- producing a given output at minimum cost for example using generics, appropriate prescribing, avoiding unnecessary hospitalization<br><br>Allocative efficiency:<br>- spending on the right mix of services given the burden of disease for example investing in primary care when most mortality is preventable at that level |
