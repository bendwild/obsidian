# Reflections
> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[Tuck and Yang 2012 Decolonization is not a metaphor.pdf#page=2&annotation=265R|Tuck and Yang 2012 Decolonization is not a metaphor, p.2]]
> > One trend we have noticed, with growing apprehension, is the ease with which the language of decolonization has been superficially adopted into education and other social sciences, supplanting prior ways of talking about social justice, critical methodologies, or approaches which  decenter settler perspectives. Decolonization, which we assert is a distinct project from other civil and human rights-based social justice projects, is far too often subsumed into the directives of these projects, with no regard for how decolonization wants something different than those forms of justice.
> >

> [!PDF|255, 208, 0] [[Tuck and Yang 2012 Decolonization is not a metaphor.pdf#page=3&annotation=268R|Tuck and Yang 2012 Decolonization is not a metaphor, p.3]]
> > The  easy  absorption,  adoption,  and transposing of decolonization is yet another form of settler appropriation.  When we write about decolonization, we are not offering it as a metaphor; it is not an approximation of other experiences of oppression.  Decolonization is not a swappable term for other things we want to do to improve our societies  and schools.  Decolonization doesn’t have a synonym
