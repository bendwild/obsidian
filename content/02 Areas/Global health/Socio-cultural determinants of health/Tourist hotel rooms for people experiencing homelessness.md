> > Viropolitics, in early COVID-19, functioned as a top-down management of pop- ulations fueled by pandemic emergency conditions: maneuvering quickly and often haphazardly, responding to rapidly changing assessments of economic and health indicators. At the same time, a related pragmatic logic emerged, spurring PEH and their advocates to capitalize on the pandemic con- ditions and demand better, more humane resources and conditions. The SIP intervention enshrined a limited citizenship on the usually marginalized and excluded: a right not to die from COVID-19; while protection from other forms of death, such as overdose, exposure, or violence, was not similarly prioritized

> [!PDF|234, 82, 82] [[SCHOEN~1.pdf#page=5&annotation=415R|SCHOEN~1, p.5]]
> > Economist Marianna Mazzucato (2018) challenges the normative characterization of government spending as pure expenditure. Highlighting a long his- tory of public investments undergirding later private developments, including the internet, cell phones, and pharmaceuticals, Mazzucato argues that value generated from initial government investment is “public value,” but this value is unrecognized in favor of strict market-based value calculations. Valua- tion, following Ecks and Mazzucato, exceeds strict market logics of fiscal responsibility and resource uti- lization. Instead, value may emerge from the body, and embodied value may also represent public value.
