# Types of sampling
The type of sampling you will do depends on the various [[Lines of reasoning in research]]

|                                  | Pro                                                                                                               | Con                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Simple random sampling**       | - easy implementation<br>- easy analysis                                                                          | - chance to miss rare subgroups (not enough people to analyze)<br>- often very expensive<br>- need for comprehensive sampling frame                                                          |
| **Systematic sampling**          | - convenient if you are not sure of the list in advance                                                           | - CAVE pattern to the sequence that you did not anticipate                                                                                                                                   |
| **Stratified sampling**          | - improves ability to study diversity and heterogeneity<br>- if you wish to draw inferences about small subgroups |                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Cluster/Multi-stage sampling** | - no need for complete sampling frame in advance<br>- often much more logistically efficient                      | - you must have a comprehensive, mutually exclusive set of clusters<br>- you must be able to identify/create a sampling frame within each selected cluster<br>- statistically less efficient |
| **Respondent-driven sampling**   | - final sample is (in theory)<br>independent of seed characteristics and representative of target<br>population.  | - only for sampling populations with a contact pattern<br>- the trait-defining membership in the population must be<br>verified objectively                                                  |
|                                  |                                                                                                                   |                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## Nomothetic approach
**= probability sampling** (random)
- lets you **generalize** to the population, allows investigators to draw inferences about a large population
- each individual in the population has an **equal probability of being selected as part of the sample**
	- all population members should have the potential to be included in the sample
	- [[Bias]] in the selection of participants should be actively prevented

> [!ATTENTION] CAVE
> May not be able to fully achieve this goal since not everyone picked will necessarily agree to participate

> [!INFO] [[Sample size calculation for analytic studies]]
> = based on effect sizes and statistical power
> - ![[Pasted image 20251101225433.png]] (example RCT)

### Simple random sampling
 = draw one random sample from across the population, most valuable when: 
 - primary interest is understanding the distribution of a characteristic across the whole population
 - the characteristic of interest DOES NOT include a variation that is rare
 - you have less interest in studying the various subgroups in the population

> [!ATTENTION] If you have a comprehensive sampling frame!

### Stratified random sampling
= sampling systematically from different parts/strata of the population, most valuable when
- research knows in advance something about how members of the population differ
- you want to ensure diversity in your population
- you want to explore differences among the different strata
- when you have rare attributes in your sample

### Systematic sampling
- decide how many people you want to be in your sample 
- calculate sampling fraction: sample size/size of whole population, 1/x
- take every x th person from the list of the target population

### Cluster/Multi-stage sampling
- organize everyone into clusters
	- **mutually exclusive** (no person in the population can be in two clusters)
	- **comprehensive** (every person in the population must be in one cluster)
- sample clusters, then within selected clusters, sample people
- 2 types
	- simple multi-stage sampling
	- multi-stage sampling proportionate to size

### Respondent driven sampling
- extension of chain-referral methods
- leverages peer networks to locate individuals in [[hidden population]]
- **dual incentives** drive participation
- various assumptions: 
	1. Accurate reporting of network size. 
	2. The population is sufficiently connected/networked 
	3. Respondents randomly recruit from their personal network.
		1. Minimal homophily
	4. Network connections are reciprocated. 
	5. Final sample composition is independent of the initial seeds.
		1. Sufficient waves of recruitment/long chains
	6. Process of being recruited creates trust and reduces underreporting

> [!attention] RDS v. Snowball sampling
> 1. Dual incentive system 
> 2. Participants not asked to identify their peers to investigator (instead, recruit directly) 
> 3. Recruitment potential/quotas 
> 4. Recruitment tracking 
> 5. Ascertainment of network size

## Idiographic approach
**= non-probability sampling** (non-random)
- no generalizing!!

> [!INFO] Sample size calculations for qualitative studies
> = based on **saturation**
> - “how many” is not the issue
> - most often between 20-40 participant
### Convenience sampling
= sampling based on ease and/or low-cost expense in obtaining that sample
- most ideal when the **characteristic of interest is thought/known to be relatively homogeneous across the population**

> [!ATTENTION] CAVE
> - convenience sampling frequently gets used in nomothetic research studies too, whether this is appropriate or not depends on the circumstances
> - however, **remember**, convenience sampling is fundamentally a non-probability sampling approach

### Purposive sampling
= intentionally selecting participants, usually with an interest in ensuring diversity of characteristics
= quota sampling

### Snowball sampling
= identifying initial study participants and asking these participant to refer others they know to the study (hidden populations f.e.)

> [!attention] RDS v. Snowball sampling
> 1. Dual incentive system 
> 2. Participants not asked to identify their peers to investigator (instead, recruit directly) 
> 3. Recruitment potential/quotas 
> 4. Recruitment tracking 
> 5. Ascertainment of network size

### Theoretical sampling
= sampling based on information being learned in the course of [[data collection]] and preliminary analyses
- iterative
- intended sample tends to evolve over the course of the study
