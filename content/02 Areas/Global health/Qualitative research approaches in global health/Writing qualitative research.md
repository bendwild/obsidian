# General tips
1. **Write to the message**, not the topic
	1. Your manuscript needs to tell a story!!
	2. There needs to be one clear message (focus on the number one important finding)
2. You first need to know your results, start the writing from there
3. Follow directions from journals!

# What to report?
The task is not to show that you know everything about the topic (≠ dissertation). You, **the researcher**, must decide what to include based on: 
- research aims
- theory
- findings
- audience
- dissemination platform

> [!ATTENTION] Remember the purpose of **idiographic research**
> - to characterize and describe
> - to study specific cases
> - to inductively derive findings
> - NOT TO measure, establish frequencies or claim universality of the findings

> [!info] Role of the [[Framework analysis]] table
> - helps to identify and visualize patterns
> - facilitates identification of interviews that have content relevant to specific codes
> - makes it easier to compare and contrast cases
> - DOES NOT determine which findings to present, nor how to best present these findings

# How to report 
There are multiple ways to present your findings. **Choose a strategy that suits your data!** The ideal sequencing of results will depend on the nature of the data and the points you want to make. 

**Tables and figures**
- supplement and enhance your story (visually)
- can be theoretical, descriptive or analytical
- don’t overdo it, use them in a way that enhances your writing

**Quotes**
- provide evidence to support your findings
- help enrich a reader’s understanding
- should be able to **stand on its own**
- needs to include enough transcript but also not be too long
- clean up verbal utterances, false starts and disruptions

> [!ATTENTION] What to avoid when quoting?
> - very short/long quotes
> - quotes that don’t give enough context
> - quotes that require extensive explanations
> - quotes that are over-edited

**Results**
- potential ways to organize findings
	- chronological
	- case studies
	- typology/theory
	- themes
- common challenges
	- repetition of information
	- including statements that should go in discussion section (e.g. implications)
	- trying to pack too much information into the results (too broad for depth)

**Paragraphs** (SQC)
- setup
- quote
- comment
