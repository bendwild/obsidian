> = a social science and study of health and health care from the economic perspective

# Core concepts

| Concept                   | Definition                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scarcity                  | Resources are limited                                                                                                                        |
| Substitutability          | There are multiple ways to achieve the same health outcomes. Think about the best alternative use of resources                               |
| Opportunity cost          | The benefits given up in the best alternative use of the resources. Using resources for one purpose precludes their use for another purpose. |
| Rational decision-making  | People weigh costs and benefits of choices and pick<br>the one that makes them best off                                                      |
| Heterogeneous preferences | People like different things (facilitates trade of goods & services)                                                                         |
| Incentives                | People respond to things perceived to affect the costs and benefits of a given choice, whether it’s monetary or not                          |

# Market failures

> [!ATTENTION] ≠ [[Perfect competition]]; violates several assumptions

1. Few buyers and few sellers
2. No homogenous products
3. No complete information
4. Barriers to entry and exit
5. **Externalities**! (second hand smoking is a negative externality f.e.)
6. Free riders

> [!ATTENTION] Uncertainty is central
> - demand for healthcare?
> - treatment effectiveness?
> - time horizon?
> - influence of genetics, biology, behavior?

# Demand for healthcare

## Characteristics

|                       | Demand                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Example                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Price                 | [[Price elasticity]] varies by type of service and type of illness<br>- price as a signal of quality **does not** increase use<br>- sunk cost **does not** increase use<br>- subsidized prices do not lead to lower willingness to pay later<br>- selection/screening/targeting: price is not effective at targeting those who need it the most | Preventative healthcare products are **extremely [[Price elasticity\|price elastic]]**<br>- deworming<br>- insecticide treated bednets<br>- point-of-use chlorine<br>- soap |
| Distance              | - demand is highly sensitive to distance!<br>- distance elasticity of demand depends on demographics                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | - [[HIV]] door-to-door free testing<br>- water source protection<br>- iron-fortified flour                                                                                      |
| Income                | Demand for healthcare is income-elastic<br>- cash transfer effectiveness debated (sustained impacts not proven)<br>- cash transfers can increase child health                                                                                                                                                                                   |                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Liquidity constraints | Alleviating liquidity constraints may increase demand<br>- more time to pay<br>- providing cash<br>- access to credit<br><br>Microfinancing has **no impact** on health expenditure                                                                                                                                                             |                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Information           | When healthy behavior comes at a cost, people often need to experiment or see others experiment to become convinced about the health returns of that behavior/technology                                                                                                                                                                        | - [[malaria]] education program<br>- water contamination programs<br>- [[HIV]]                                                                                                  |
| Social influences     | - higher WTP if peers adopt<br>- social signalling beneficial                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | - bednets<br>- menstrual cups<br>- deworming<br>- chlorine                                                                                                                  |
## Policy interventions

|                    | Demand                                                                                                                                                                         | Examples                                              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| User fees          | Demand for healthcare is income-elastic<br>- removal of user fees removes deterrent effect<br>- less discrepancies in usage rate between rich and poor                         |                                                       |
| Incentives         | Effective at increasing demand<br>- as a price subsidy<br>- increase extrinsic motivation<br>- compensate for present-[[bias]]<br>- counter/exploit loss-aversion<br>- as a signal | Lottery Incentives to incentivize safe sex in Lesotho |
| Universal coverage |                                                                                                                                                                                |                                                       |
