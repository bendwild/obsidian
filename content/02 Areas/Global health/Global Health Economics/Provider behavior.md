= principal-agent problem (*for whose interest are health providers working for?*)

# Fundamental problems
Healthcare providers do not work in a [[perfect competition]]
1. **Information asymmetries** (the health provider may exploit their special knowledge for their own benefit)
	1. allows them to manipulate prices & quantity
2. Health providers as **monopolists** (with control over the price the provider can charge the full price the market will bear)
3. Health providers **inducing demand** (use their power as advisors to push more services than may be needed)
4. **Target income** hypothesis (in response to lower income, physician induce demand and push more services at higher prices)

**Provider behavior is affected by payments**

|                                | What?                                                                                                                                      | Pro                                                                                                                                                         | Con                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Salary income**              | Fixed and regular payment                                                                                                                  | - relies on **intrinsic motivation**<br>- ensures **minimum take-home pay**<br>- can result in **highly varied performanc**e (no marginal cost to provider) | - needs performance and accountability systems<br>- needs to ensure salaries are competitive and perceived to be appropiate                                                       |
| **Fee-for-service**            | Reimburse workers for each service provided                                                                                                | - may encourage **under-provision (monopoly situation) or over-provision (inelastic demand)**<br>- less accountability for **qualit**y                      | - managed care organizations to shop for patients and restore competition<br>- pre-authorizations and case management reviews for “medical necessity”<br>- performance monitoring |
| **Capitation**                 | Prospectively paid a fixed amount per member per month, employer keeps any savings below total payment                                     | - incentives efficiency, **but only to the point of capacity**                                                                                              | - substitute towards cheaper services <br>- reduce hospital admissions or lengths of stay<br>- task-shifting to cheaper skills mix<br>- reduced access to care for patients       |
| **Performance-based payments** | Payment based on meeting agree-upon performance targets<br>- pay-for-performance<br>- bundled payments<br>- accountable care organizations |                                                                                                                                                             | - requires monitoring of performance outcomes<br>- needs to be carefully designed to get desired outcomes<br>- requires buy-in from providers, teams, and managers                |

> [!attention] Optimal payment structure is a mix of all of the above
