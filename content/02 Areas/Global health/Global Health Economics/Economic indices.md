# Price measurement indices
## Consumer Price Index (CPI)
= measures changes in the price of a **basket of goods and services** over time
= indexes the real value of some quantity for price inflation

![[Pasted image 20260122113224.png]]

CPI1 usually 100
Comparison year usually 1

## Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
= commercial exchange rates (CER) reflect subjective international desirability, including usefulness for trade
= accounts for **different living standards** (different prices) in different countries (comparing the cost of a typical basket of goods in each country)
= more useful than exchange rate

Example:
- CER US$1 = 72 Indian Rupee.
- PPP-adjusted: US$1 = 18 IR (to buy the same basket of goods)
- Indian wage is 50,000 IR/month.

(Is this wage more equivalent to US$700 (based on CER), or US$2,778 (based on PPP)?)

> [!ATTENTION] CAVE
> Works well for general consumer goods, often inaccurate for healthcare since variation is larger

# National economic indicators

## Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
= common measure for expenditures
= total monetary value of all goods and services produced by a nation during a year (excluding any income from foreign investments)
= private consumption + gross investment + government spending + (exports - imports)

| Strengths                                                                                                                                                | Weaknesses                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| - easy to calculate<br>- universally implemented<br>- *may* move in tandem with a nation’s living standards<br>- ample historical data on country trends | - excludes informal sector work, subsistence farming, illegal activity<br>- excludes non-market transactions (home production, volunteer work)<br>- all economic activity is counted, even if odious (war, oil spills)<br>- GDP doesn’t account for quality of goods<br>- GDP ignores income distribution |

## Gross National Income (GNI)
= GDP plus income received from overseas sources minus income earned by non-residents
**= calculates income instead of outcome**

## Human Development Index (HDI)
= long and health life (life expectancy at birth) + knowledge (expected years of schooling & mean years of schooling) + decent standard of living (GNI)

## Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI)
= combines 26 different variables including environmental and social factors 
- income weighted private consumption
- non-market services generating welfare
- private defensive cost of natural deterioration
- cost of deterioration of nature and natural resource
- increase in capital stock and balance of international trade
