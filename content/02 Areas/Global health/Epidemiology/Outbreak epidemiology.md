> [!INFO] Outbreak = occurrence of more cases of a disease than expected (for a particular place and time)
> - OPEN outbreak setting = **ALL** members of a community in which disease transmission is occurring are also involved in outside groups
> - CONTAINED outbreak setting = **MOST** members do NOT leave the setting where disease transmission is occurring (e.g. nursing homes, correctional facilities)

> [!INFO] Goals of outbreak [[epidemiology]]
> - reduce transmission
> - reduce illness, complications, and death
> - evaluate existing prevention strategies
> - inform future prevention strategies
> - address public concern

|                  | Outbreak [[epidemiology]]                                                                                                                                               | Standard research [[epidemiology]]                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Purpose**          | Control transmission **NOW**                                                                                                                                        | Produce generalizable evidence                 |
| **Priority**         | **Speed** over perfection                                                                                                                                           | Rigor over speed                               |
| **Data**             | Works with **incomplete/evolving data**                                                                                                                             | Uses clean, stable, validated datasets         |
| **Case definitions** | Case definitions change as more is learned                                                                                                                          | **Fixed, standardized** case definitions       |
| **Tools**            | Uses **rapid tools**: <br>- line lists<br>- epi curves<br>- spot maps<br><br>Heavy field work<br>- case finding<br>- contact tracing<br>- environmental assessments | Emphasis on [[study design]] & confounding control |
| **Duration**         | Decisions required in hours - days                                                                                                                                  | Analyses performed over months - years         |
| **Success**          | = **interruption of transmission**                                                                                                                                  | = valid, reproducible findings                 |

# Outbreak management
## 1. Prevent
- mitigation plans
- infection prevention and control
- communication with local health department

## 2. Identify
- identify cases ([[disease surveillance]])
- link to a setting
- set a threshold

## 3. Define the scope
- who, when, where?
	- people are linked by many factors, find out the network
	- location-based investigation
- case finding
	- everyone is **epidemiologically-linked** (a person who is connected to a confirmed/probable case by person, place and time and is considered infected based on exposure)
	- identify exposed persons (**contacts**)
- [[data collection]]
	- line list (information about identified cases)
		= key tool in managing an outbreak
- descriptive [[epidemiology]]
	- spot map (place)
	- epidemic curve (time)
	
> [!info] Case investigation/contact tracing is useful for: 
> - [[tuberculose]]
> - measles
> - [[SOI]]
> - ebola
> - norovirus
> - mpox
> - *corona* (helpful **BUT** too widespread and variants move too fast (omicron))

## 4. Control
- test, trace, isolate
	- **break the chains of transmission**
	- phylogenetic analysis (find out who infected who based on the strain of the virus)
	- **targeted** testing (most common) vs **response-driven** testing
- prevention measures
	- ring vaccination
	- cohorts/pods/stable groups (red, yellow, green zones)
- closures/restrictions

> [!INFO] The outbreak managers role in controlling outbreaks
> - assess implementation of a plan and identify gaps
> - advise on testing, cohorting, closures and other restrctions
> - facilitate thorough CI/CT (line lists)
> - link facilities with resources
> - monitor, follow-up and advise when restrictions  can be rleaxed

## 5. Monitor
- ongoing reporting
- follow-up
- assess control measures

| Indicator                                              | Numerator                                                                  | Denominator                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cumulative Incidence  <br>(or attack rate or risk)** | Number of new cases of disease during specified time interval              | Population at start of time interval                              |
| **Secondary attack rate**                              | Number of new cases among contacts                                         | Total number of contacts                                          |
| **Point prevalence**                                   | Number of current cases (new and preexisting) at a specified point in time | Population at the same specified point in time                    |
| **Positivity Rate**<br>                                | Number of positive test results in given time period                       | Number of positive and negative test results in given time period |
see [[epidemiologist bathtub]]

> [!ATTENTION] 5% threshold for high positivity rate
> - suggests need for expanded testing
> - suggests need for increased intervention/prevention
