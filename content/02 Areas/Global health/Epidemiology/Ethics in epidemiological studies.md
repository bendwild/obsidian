# Why?
- [[epidemiology]] often **shapes public policy**
- epidemiological studies are generally **funded from public resources**
- have societal relevance
- people who participate in studies **usually do not directly gain from the study results**

# How
## Ethical principles for the protection of human subjects
= Belmont Report
- **respect for persons**
	- autonomy
	- protection for persons with diminished autonomy
- **beneficence**
	- maximize possible benefits
	- do not harm/minimize possible known harms
- **justice**
	- who bears the burden and who receive the benefits (f.e. drug development in LMIC)

## Review by IRB
Any research **involving human subjects** in which: 
- intervening or interacting with a human subject
- obtaining personal identifying information

> [!INFO] Examples of IRB exemptions (in theory)
> - publicly available (de-identified) data sets (f.e. NHANES)
> - research on organizations
> - journalism
> - coded private information or biological specimens
> - literature reviews
> - public health programs for disease investigation, surveillance, performance evaluation

> [!INFO] A typical IRB application
> - study objectives
> 	- hypothesis?
> 	- why is it important
> - [[study area]] and methods
> 	- be very specific, describe each step of your study procedure
> - target population & inclusion/exclusion criteria
> - subject recruitment procedure
> 	- be very specific (who, where, etc.)
> - [[data collection]] tools and methods
> - balance of harm and benefit 
> 	- **almost always some risk** (e.g. loss of confidentiality), ask yourself the question if the benefits outweigh the risk
> 	- compensation ≠ benefits (societal benefits?)
> - protection of privacy
> - consent and compensation
> 	- how much is too much/how much is too little (CAVE bribing)
> 	- informed consent form 

## Informed consent
Essential components of IC form: 
- purpose and background
- invitation to participate
- study procedure
- possible benefits
- possible risks
- confidentiality
## HIPPA
= [[health insurance]] portability and accountability act
- limits release of protected health information (PHI) without authorization of patient
	- name
	- address
	- SSN
	- doctor’s/nurse’s personal notes
	- billing information
- PHI can be released **without authorization of patient** for public health activities related to **disease prevention/control**

> [!ATTENTION] HIPPA does NOT apply to international studies
> - best to still follow HIPPA guidelines even though they don’t legally apply

## Conflicts of interest
= researchers **must state any conflicts of interest**
- funding sources
- personal conflicts of interest
- contractual
- professional
