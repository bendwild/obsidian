> = a severe childbirth injury, usually caused by **prolonged obstructed labor**
> - results in an abnormal connection (fistula) between:
> 	- vagina and bladder (vesicovaginal)
> 	- or vagina and rectum (rectovaginal)
> - leads to **continuous leakage of urine or feces** and major physical + social consequences.
    
# Epidemiology
 Difficult to measure (rare + stigmatized condition), but estimates point at: 
- ~500,000 women living with fistula globally
- 50,000–100,000 new cases/year
	- most cases occur in **sub-Saharan Africa**.
	- increasing **iatrogenic fistula** (caused by surgical error) linked to rising C-section rates.
# Etiology
Occurs when the baby cannot pass through the birth canal despite contractions.
- Major causes:
    - Cephalopelvic disproportion (baby too large / pelvis too small)
    - Malpresentation (abnormal fetal position)
- Accounts for:
    - ~3% of maternal deaths globally
    - 1.7–7% of deliveries in sub-Saharan Africa
- Preventable with **timely surgical intervention (C-section, forceps, etc.)**

# Symptoms

| Physical                                                                                                                                  | Social                                            | Psychological                                 | Fetal                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| - urinary/fecal incontinence<br>- chronic infections, kidney damage<br>- infertility, vaginal stenosis<br>- nerve damage (e.g. foot drop) | - stigma<br>- isolation<br>- divorce<br>- poverty | - [[depressie]]<br>- low self-esteem<br>- suicide | ~95% fetal death rate in obstructed labor cases |
# Treatment 

- **Fistula repair surgery** is the main treatment.
- Access to surgery has improved globally.
- However, surgery alone is **not sufficient for full recovery**.
	- Reintegration vs Rehabilitation

## Post-surgery recovery
### Persistent issues:
- Some women still experience:
    - Incontinence
    - Pain and mobility problems
    - Economic hardship

> [!important] Recovery is gradual and incomplete for many

### Reintegration & Rehabilitation
> - **Reintegration**: Returning to normal social, economic, and community life
> - **Rehabilitation**: Interventions to restore function and reduce disability

#### How?
- Counseling (mental health)
- Health education
- Physical therapy
- Social support
- Economic empowerment (skills, income)

> [!ATTENTION] Programmatic gaps
> - Limited evidence on what works best
> - Most programs:
> 	- Are small and facility-based
> 	- Lack strong evaluation designs
> - Barriers: 
> 	- Funding shortages
> 	- Weak coordination
> 	- Limited research and data

# [[Global health priorities]]

## Research:
- Evaluate effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of interventions

## Programs:
- Develop **holistic, patient-centered care**

## Policy:
- Increase funding across the entire care continuum

## Collaboration:
- Strengthen global coordination and evidence sharing

> [!important] Main take-aways
> - Fistula is **preventable** but persists due to weak health systems.
> - It is not just a medical issue → **social and economic dimensions are critical**.
> - Surgery is necessary but **not sufficient** → long-term rehabilitation is essential.
> - There is a major **gap in evidence-based reintegration programs**.
