# Paradigms

> Division between communicable and non-communicable diseases actually does not make sense. There is futility in pitting communicable and noncommunicable diseases against one another (infectious disease prevention reduces NCDs f.e.)

| Myth                                                                               | Fact                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Infectious diseases are acute and deadly, NCDs are chronic                         | Many infectious diseases have chronic sequelae, and some NCDs may not result in chronic illness                                           |
| In LMICs infectious diseases are large drivers of mortality, in comparison to NCDs | Epidemiologic transition: <br>- NCDs account for the majority of deaths worldwide<br>- there is a disproportionate burden of NCDs in LMIC |
| Infectious diseases and NCDs are two separate entities                             | Infectious diseases can be triggers for NCDs through: <br>- direct tissue damage<br>- immune dysregulation<br>- immune-mediated reaction  |

> [!IMPORTANT] The “Big Four”
> - Cardiovascular diseases
> - Diabetes
> - Cancer
> - Chronic respiratory diseases


# Epidemiologic transition

| Environment                                                                                                          | Health system                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| - [[tobacco]] use expansion into LMIC markets<br>- ultra-processed foods <br>- sedentary work patterns<br>- urbanization | - acute and episodic care models<br>- vertical disease programs<br>- limited chronic continuity care |

> [!ATTENTION] Why are we not prioritizing NCDs?
> - Donor priorities historically favored infectious diseases
> - Measurement challenges
> - Lack of short-term wins
