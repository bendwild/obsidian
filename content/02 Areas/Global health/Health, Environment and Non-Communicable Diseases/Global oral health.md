> [!ATTENTION] Oral disease and health
> - oral health is essential to general health and quality of life
> - poor oral health has impacts beyond one anatomical region of the body and can substantially restrict daily functioning and have a profound effect on quality of life and overall wellbeing


# Epidemiology
- [[oral diseases]] affect **more people worldwide than any other health condition**
- more people suffer from untreated tooth decay than are afflicted with heart disease, cancer, diabetes, asthma, or upper respiratory infections... combined

# Main diseases

|                  | Dental caries                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Periodontal diseases                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Oral cancer                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Etiology**     | - acid-producing oral bacteria ferment dietary carbohydrates<br>- persistent acid exposure demineralizes tooth structure<br>- saliva helps clear food and buffer acids<br>- bacteria biofilm disease with chronic course | - inflammation in response to subgingival bacteria, causing<br>destruction of tooth-supporting structures<br>- strongly associated with other chronic and inflammatory<br>conditions ([[Diabetes mellitus]], CVD, preterm birth…) | - [[tobacco]] & alcohol established risk factors<br>- HPV infection increasingly important                                          |
| **Epidemiology** | - individuals susceptible throughout the life course<br>- ~90% of world population will experience tooth decay<br>- **most common chronic health condition**                                                             |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | 6th most common cancer worldwide<br>- extreme variable incidence by country/region<br>- highest prevalence in South(-east) Asia |
| **Symptoms**     | - tooth decay                                                                                                                                                                                                            | - affects supporting structures of teeth<br>- can lead to tooth loss                                                                                                                                                              | Often detected at late stage<br>- treatment is highly destructive (resection and radiation)<br>- 5-year survival +/- 50%        |

> [!info] Dilemma
> Theoretically, dental caries is an infectious disease (bacterial fermenting), but since basically everyone has it we generally think of it as a cronic NCD

# Treatment
- Dental care is the health need most likely not met due to cost
- Too many dental conditions go through the emergency department
- Medicine and dentistry in separate spherese
	- less than 1/3 of dentists accept public insurance
	- Medicare, and most adult Medicaid programs, and many private health plans exclude dental care

# Reducing the burden of [[oral diseases]]
1. Surveillance, monitoring and evaluation
2. Reduce dietary sugar intake, and [[tobacco]] usage
3. Access to affordable and effective fluoride
	1. Community fluoridation
	2. Fluoride varnish
	3. Sliver diamine fluoride
4. Population wide approaches
	1. High cost, interventionist, “drill + fill” dentistry does not address the causes of oral disease and is **unaffordable and inaccessible** to the vast majority of the world’s population
	2. Dental therapists (middle-elevel provider)
	3. Promoting healthy behaviors
